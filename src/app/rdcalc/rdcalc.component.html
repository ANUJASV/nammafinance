What is an RD?

A Recurring Deposit (RD) is a savings scheme offered by banks and post offices that allows you to deposit a fixed amount every month for a specified period and earn interest on it. Unlike a Fixed Deposit (FD), where you invest a lump sum once, an RD helps inculcate a habit of regular saving by contributing monthly, making it ideal for salaried individuals or those with steady income.

RDs offer guaranteed returns with virtually zero risk and are a great way to build a disciplined savings routine.

How a RD Works?

You choose an RD tenure (ranging typically from 6 months to 10 years) and commit to depositing a fixed amount every month. Each installment earns interest, and the total amount plus interest is paid at maturity.
The interest rate is fixed at the time of account opening, and compounding (usually quarterly) boosts your earnings.

maturity amount = Sum of all monthly deposits + interest accrued.

Types of Recurring Deposits

  • Regular RD – Standard monthly deposit RD available for all individuals.
  • Senior Citizen RD – Higher interest rates offered to those above 60 years.
  • Post Office RD – 5-year RD with government-backed safety and fixed interest.
  • Flexi RD – Allows variable monthly deposits (offered by some banks).
  • RD for Minors – Jointly opened with a guardian to help children start saving early.

How RD Interest is Calculated?

Interest on RDs is compounded quarterly, unlike FDs where a lump sum earns compound interest from the start.
Each monthly deposit earns interest for the remaining tenure (e.g., the first deposit earns interest for the full term, the second for one month less, and so on).

How is RD Taxed?

  • Interest earned on RDs is fully taxable under "Income from Other Sources".
  • No TDS is deducted by default (unlike FDs), but interest must be reported while filing your income tax return (ITR).
  • If the interest crosses the exemption limit or tax liability arises, advance tax must be paid.
